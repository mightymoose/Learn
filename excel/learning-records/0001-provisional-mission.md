# The Excel mission is a guess, and four questions are open

Ryan asked for an Excel course for Carla on 2026-08-14. Carla was not in the session, so
nobody interviewed her. The mission was written from an assumption about a common
workplace goal. It is marked provisional in [MISSION.md](../MISSION.md).

This matters for the next session. The teaching skill treats the mission as the thing
that decides what to teach next. A guessed mission cannot do that job. Confirm it before
tier 2 is written.

## The four open questions

1. **Why does Carla want this?** What does she do at work, and what is she blocked on
   today? The answer decides the worked examples in every lesson.
2. **What Excel does she run?** Microsoft 365, or 2021, 2024, 2019, 2016. Windows or
   Mac. This decides whether lesson 5 teaches XLOOKUP or INDEX and MATCH. See
   [NOTES.md](../NOTES.md).
3. **What does she already do in Excel?** "Beginner" covers a wide range. Someone who
   types numbers into a shared file is not the same as someone who writes SUM.
4. **How far does "expert" go?** The roadmap runs to Power Query, DAX and automation.
   That is a lot of ground. Some of it may be out of scope for her.

## What was built without the answers

Tier 1, four lessons, plus a roadmap card and two reference cards. Tier 1 was chosen
because it is invariant. A beginner needs the grid, references, Tables and aggregation
whatever the answers turn out to be, and the lessons use no feature that differs between
versions. So a wrong guess costs nothing already written.

Tiers 2 to 4 exist as a roadmap only, deliberately. Writing them now would bake in the
guess.

## Implications

- Do not write lesson 5 until question 2 is answered.
- When the answers arrive, rewrite [MISSION.md](../MISSION.md) and supersede this record.
- If Carla's real goal turns out to be narrow, cut tiers rather than teaching all four.
