Lesson 02 · Tier 1, Foundations

The dollar sign

Write a formula once and fill it down a thousand rows. One character decides whether that works or quietly produces nonsense.

After this lesson

You can write a single formula, copy it across a whole block, and get the right answer in every cell. You can also look at a broken column of numbers and say which reference should have been locked.

Copying a formula moves its references

First, set C2 back to 120, so the data matches lesson 1 again.

In E2 you have =C2*D2. Copy that cell and paste it into E3. Look at the formula bar. It does not say =C2*D2. It says =C3*D3.

Excel did not copy the text. It copied the relationship. Your formula meant two cells to my left, multiplied, and that is what moved down with it.

This is called a relative reference, and it is the default. It is also the reason you can write one formula and fill a column. Grab the small square at the bottom right corner of the cell and drag it down, or select the cell and press Ctrl D after selecting the range below it.

Sometimes moving is exactly wrong

Now add commission. The rate is 8 percent. Following the rule from lesson 1, the rate goes in its own cell rather than inside a formula. Put the label in G1 and the number 0.08 in H1.

In F2, the obvious formula is this.

=E2*H1

It gives 43.20, which is right. Fill it down to F7 and the column falls apart. F3 shows 0. So do F4 through F7.

Click F3 and read the formula bar. It says =E3*H2. The revenue reference moved down correctly. The rate reference moved down too, and H2 is empty. Multiplying by an empty cell gives zero.

Both references moved, because both were relative. You wanted only one of them to.

You wrote =E2*H1 in F2 and filled it down to F7. Every row below the first shows zero. Why?

The dollar sign pins a reference

A dollar sign locks whatever comes immediately after it. That is the entire rule.

FormNameWhat moves when you copy it
H1RelativeBoth the column and the row move
$H$1AbsoluteNothing moves. It always points at H1
$H1MixedColumn stays H, row moves
H$1MixedRow stays 1, column moves

So the commission formula should be this.

=E2*$H$1

Fill that down and every row is right, because $H$1 refuses to move while E2 happily does.

The keystroke

You do not have to type dollar signs. Click into the formula, put the cursor on a reference, and press F4. It cycles H1, $H$1, H$1, $H1 and back. On many Macs this is Command T, and on some laptops you need Fn F4.

When you only want to lock half

Full locking with $H$1 covers most beginner cases. The mixed forms matter the moment you fill a formula both down and across, which is what a grid of rates or a multiplication table needs.

The test is simple. Ask each part of the reference separately: as this formula moves, should the column letter change? Should the row number change? Put a dollar sign in front of each answer that is no.

You need a reference that always points at column B, but moves down the rows as you fill. Which form is it?

Do this now

Set C2 back to 120. Put Comm rate in G1 and 0.08 in H1.

In F2 type =E2*H1. Fill it down to F7 and look at the zeros. See the failure once, deliberately.

Click F2, click on H1 in the formula bar, press F4. Press Enter. Fill down again. All six rows are now right.

Change H1 to 0.10. All six commissions update at once. That is what the whole lesson bought you.

A filled column is giving wrong answers. Put the diagnosis in order.

Ask your teacher. Good questions here: when a mixed reference is genuinely needed, why F4 does nothing on your keyboard, or what happens to a dollar-signed reference when somebody inserts a row above it.
