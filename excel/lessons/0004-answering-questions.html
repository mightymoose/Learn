Lesson 04 · Tier 1, Foundations

Answering questions

Adding up everything is easy. Adding up only the rows that match a condition is the first thing that feels like analysis.

After this lesson

You can answer "how much did the North region sell" with a formula instead of by filtering and squinting at the status bar. You can build a small summary block that updates itself, and you can change the question without rewriting the formula.

The manual way, and why it does not last

You already have a Table called Sales with a Revenue column. To find North's revenue you could click the filter arrow, tick North, and read the sum off the bottom of the window.

That works exactly once. It leaves no record, it cannot be checked by anyone else, and tomorrow you have to do it again. Nothing about it survives a new month of data.

What you want is a cell that permanently means North's revenue.

SUMIFS

The function is SUMIFS. Read the name as sum, if these conditions hold. Put this somewhere outside the Table.

=SUMIFS(Sales[Revenue], Sales[Region], "North")

It returns 1290, which is Ana's 540 plus Cara's 750. Read the arguments in order.

ArgumentMeaning
Sales[Revenue]The column to add up. This one comes first.
Sales[Region]The column to test.
"North"What it has to equal.

The last two arguments come as a pair, and you can keep adding pairs. To ask for North's revenue at the higher price only, add a second condition.

=SUMIFS(Sales[Revenue], Sales[Region], "North", Sales[Price], 4.5)

Every pair narrows the result further. All conditions must hold at once.

The trap

There is an older function called SUMIF, with no S, and it takes its arguments in the opposite order. Forum answers mix the two constantly. Use SUMIFS always, even for a single condition. One function, one argument order, one thing to remember.

In SUMIFS, which range do you write first?

Put the question in a cell

The formula above has "North" typed inside it. That is the mistake from lesson 1 wearing a different hat. To ask about South you would have to edit the formula.

Put the region name in its own cell instead. If H4 holds the word North, the formula becomes this.

=SUMIFS(Sales[Revenue], Sales[Region], H4)

Now change H4 to South and the answer follows. Better still, write the three region names down a column and fill the formula beside them. You have built a summary that answers all three questions at once and keeps answering them next month.

The summary block. Column H holds the questions, tinted. Columns I to K hold formulas that read them. The total matches the Table's own total row, which is how you know the block is right.

The rest of the family

The same pattern gives you counting and averaging. The condition pairs work identically, so learning one teaches you all three.

QuestionFormula
How much did North sell?=SUMIFS(Sales[Revenue], Sales[Region], H4)
How many North rows are there?=COUNTIFS(Sales[Region], H4)
What is North's average sale?=AVERAGEIFS(Sales[Revenue], Sales[Region], H4)

COUNTIFS has no range to add up, so it starts straight in on condition pairs. That is the one difference. All three are listed under Statistical functions in Excel functions by category.

Why is it better to put the word North in cell H4 than to type it inside the formula?

Check your own work

This is the habit that makes you trusted rather than merely fast.

Your three regional totals should add up to the Table's grand total. 1290 plus 787.50 plus 1350 is 3427.50, which matches the total row from lesson 3. If it did not match, a region would be missing or misspelled somewhere in the data.

Build that check into the sheet rather than doing it in your head. Put =SUM(I4:I6)-SUM(Sales[Revenue]) in a spare cell. It should read zero, and if it ever stops reading zero, something upstream changed.

Put the build of the summary block in order.

Do this now

In H4, H5 and H6, type North, South and East.

In I4 write =SUMIFS(Sales[Revenue], Sales[Region], H4) and fill it down to I6. Note that H4 moves with the fill, which is exactly what you want here, and why it has no dollar signs.

Add the COUNTIFS and AVERAGEIFS columns beside it.

Add the zero check. Then misspell one Region value in the Table on purpose and watch the check stop reading zero. Fix it again.

Ask your teacher. Good questions here: how to write a condition like "greater than 500" rather than an exact match, what to do when the criteria list should come from the data instead of being typed, or why a region total silently comes out as zero.
