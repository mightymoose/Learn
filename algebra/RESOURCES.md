# Abstract Algebra Resources

Every link below was opened and checked in the session that added it. If you find a dead
link, delete it rather than leaving it here.

## Knowledge

- **Serge Lang, _Algebra_, Revised 3rd ed. (Springer GTM 211, 2002)**, at `sources/Serge Lang - Algebra.pdf`
  The source text for this workspace. ISBN 0-387-95385-X. 934 scanned pages, no text
  layer. Use for: the canonical statement of anything. Its Chapter I, §11 introduces
  categories and universal objects earlier than most first courses do.

- [Keith Conrad, "Universal Mapping Properties"](https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/linmultialg/universalmapping.pdf)
  24 pages. Works the same pattern through cyclic groups, quotient groups,
  abelianizations, direct products, quotient rings, fraction fields, and free modules,
  with an appendix on product topology and metric completion. Use for: the worked
  examples Lang compresses into a sentence.

- [Keith Conrad's expository papers (index)](https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/)
  Course-handout-quality notes across algebra and number theory. Use for: a second,
  slower explanation whenever Lang is too terse.

- [Emily Riehl, _Category Theory in Context_ (free PDF, author-hosted)](https://emilyriehl.github.io/files/context.pdf)
  Dover 2016, hosted free by the author. Covers categories, functors, natural
  transformations, Yoneda, limits and colimits, adjunctions, monads, Kan extensions.
  Use for: the category theory Lang assumes but does not develop.

- [The Stacks Project](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/)
  Open collaborative reference, 7500+ pages, maintained by Aise Johan de Jong. Every
  result has a stable tag. Use for: checking how a modern source states a result.
  Its [Categories chapter is tag 0011](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0011).

- [nLab](https://ncatlab.org/)
  Category-theoretic wiki. Use for: the abstract framing of a concept, and for finding
  the standard name of something you can only describe.

## Wisdom (Communities)

- [MathOverflow](https://mathoverflow.net)
  Research-level Q&A, founded 2009, part of the Stack Exchange network. Deliberately
  distinct from math.stackexchange. Use for: questions about a paper you are reading, or
  why a definition is stated a particular way. Read for a while before posting.

- [Mathematics Stack Exchange](https://math.stackexchange.com)
  The non-research tier. Use for: "why does this proof step work" questions that would
  be closed on MathOverflow.

## Gaps

- No community found yet for reading arXiv preprints together. A seminar or reading
  group would serve the mission better than a Q&A site. Worth searching for.
- No verified second textbook that takes a categories-first route through algebra.
  Riehl covers the category theory but is not an algebra course.
