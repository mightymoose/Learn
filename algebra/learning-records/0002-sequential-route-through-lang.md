# Route decision: cover Lang sequentially from Chapter I, §1

Ryan chose to work through Lang in the book's own order, starting at §1 on monoids,
rather than entering at the section that most directly serves the mission. He also chose
to rebuild the lesson sequence rather than keep an out-of-order lesson at number 1.

## Why this came up

The first workspace build started at Chapter I, §11 on categories and universal objects.
The reasoning was that §11 sits at the edge of a solid undergraduate floor and unblocks
reading arXiv papers fastest. Ryan pushed back, noting that Lang starts with monoids and
groups.

He was right, for a reason the original plan missed. §11 is not self-contained. Its own
examples on p. 58 use free abelian groups from §7 and the Grothendieck group, and it
lists the direct and inverse limits of §10 as universal objects. Entering there means
entering a section that cites three earlier ones.

The deeper error was mine. Ryan said "complete mastery" and also picked a starting floor
of solid undergraduate algebra. Those two answers pull in different directions, and I
resolved the tension silently instead of surfacing it.

## Implications

- Lessons follow Lang's section order. No jumping to what serves the mission fastest.
- An early section still does not get a first-course lesson. Reading p. 3 confirmed the
  monoid definition sits below Ryan's floor, so lesson 01 covers what §1 actually
  establishes, which is the product notation on pp. 4 to 6.
- Pace varies. A familiar section gets one lesson. §10 and §11 get more.
- The universal-properties reference card stays, marked as ahead of the sequence. Its
  lesson gets written when the route reaches §11.
- When a mission answer and a floor answer conflict, raise it rather than pick one.
  See [[0001-starting-point]] and [[MISSION.md]].
