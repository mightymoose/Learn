# Mission: Abstract Algebra (Lang, *Algebra*, GTM 211)

## Why

Ryan wants to read modern algebra papers on arXiv without stalling. Research papers
assume the reader already owns the vocabulary and the standard moves, so they state a
construction once and then use it. The goal is complete mastery of that assumed layer,
using Lang's *Algebra* as the source text.

## Success looks like

- Open a recent arXiv preprint in math.RA, math.AC, or math.NT and follow its first
  three pages without stopping to look up notation.
- Read the phrase "by the universal property of X" and know what was just claimed,
  without re-deriving it.
- Reconstruct a standard proof from Lang (Sylow, structure theorem, Galois
  correspondence) from memory, not from rereading.
- Say what a paper's main theorem asserts, in plain language, after one pass.

## Route

Sequential through Lang, from Chapter I, §1, covering every section in the book's order.
A lesson never reteaches the first-course version of a section. Section map in
[NOTES.md](./NOTES.md), reasoning in learning record 0002.

## Constraints

- Source text is a 934-page scan with no text layer. Every citation is checked against
  a rendered page, never from memory. See [NOTES.md](./NOTES.md) for the page offset.
- Starting floor: undergraduate algebra, mostly solid. Groups, quotients, and rings are
  familiar.
- No deadline. This is mastery work, so lessons favour long-term retention over
  coverage speed.

## Out of scope

- Exam-style speed drills. There is no qualifying exam driving this.
- Algebraic geometry and homotopy theory as subjects in their own right. They are
  downstream of the mission, not part of it.
- Reteaching definitions the floor already covers. Cover the section, not the
  first-course version of it.
